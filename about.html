Engineer Profile

The Engineer
Who Builds Software

I'm a Chemical and Process Engineer (BSc Eng Hons, University of Peradeniya) who learned to code — not a developer who learned some engineering terminology. That distinction matters more than it sounds when you're building tools for real industrial processes.

Today I work as an Engineering Executive at Haycarb PLC, a global manufacturer of coconut-shell activated carbon. Day to day that means rotary kiln activation, comminution and milling, particle size control, and the plant data behind them — direct plant-floor context that most software developers simply don't have.

What drew me to building software wasn't a career pivot — it was frustration. The tools available for process engineers are either expensive, generic, or both. So I started building my own. The roller mill PSD optimisation platform started as a personal tool. The stock counting app was built because the plant needed something the Zebra TC26 could actually run. The janitorial management system replaced a clipboard and a spreadsheet.

I'm at the start of my career — but the software I build is grounded in real process knowledge, not guesswork. If you need someone who can read your PSD data, understand why your Rosin-Rammler fit is off, and then build the tool to fix it — that's exactly what I do.
